The Rideau Regional Centre, as far as the record goes

Fifty-eight years on Highway 43, and a statistical disappearance in 1977

1
1951
Opens as the "Ontario Hospital School, Smiths Falls"
One of 16 provincial institutions in rural settings. Average resident population rose from 863 in 1953 to 2,367 in 1964, the highest year in the published series; 2,070 residents in 1971, the largest of the three that lasted.
2
1971 to 1974
The Williston review, then the Developmental Services Act
After the death and maltreatment of two men who had lived at Rideau, the province moved developmental services out of Health and committed to community alternatives.
3
1976
Last appearance in the national series
Statistics Canada's directory lists Rideau at 1,128 beds, 1,395.6 average inpatients, 123.7 percent occupancy.
4
1977
42 facilities and 14,000 beds reclassified out of the count
The national series fell 17,558 beds, 43.8 percent, in one year. The 1976 table counts exactly 42 public hospitals of this type, 28 of them in Ontario, and the 1976 directory names Rideau among them. None of the 42 appears in 1977.
5
2004 to 2009
Closure announced; closed 31 March 2009
With Huronia and Southwestern. 435 residents at Rideau when closure was announced, by a member's statement in the Legislature; about 1,000 moved out of the three centres over five years at a provincial cost of $276 million; average age 51, about 39 years in institutions. The Town's own 2020 housing report says the Centre employed more than 830 people, against Hershey's 550 at its peak.
6
2013 to 2014
Settlement and apology
$32.7 million ($20.6 million for Rideau), approved 24 February 2014, for about 2,750 former Rideau residents and 1,600 from Southwestern; individual awards from up to $2,000 to a maximum of $42,000; the Premier's written apology to Rideau and Southwestern residents on 17 April 2014.

Settlement figures are aggregates only.

Your question, and the honest state of the evidence

What the record can say, and what it cannot

check_circleIt can say

  • circleHow large the Centre was and in what category: 1,128 beds in 1976; 2,070 residents in 1971; one of three that lasted to 2009; the town's largest employer by the Town's own account.
  • circleThat the national count dropped its whole category in 1977, so the years the institutions emptied are the years the country stopped counting the beds.
  • circleThat the town carries 39.6 percent of the County's social housing with 13 percent of its people (2018), which is consistent with the Committee's account of a welcoming town.
  • circleThat the town is pressured on every pillar, with renters and low-income households first in line.
  • circleThat the pattern predates the closure. A first test across 58 Canadian towns that hosted a now-closed institution finds their renter and low-income profiles were already in place by 2001 and did not shift when the institutions closed or as they emptied. Smiths Falls had 44 percent renters in 2001, with the Centre still open. The record points to where these institutions were sited, not to what closing them did.
  • circleWhere the Centre's residents were counted. In Montague Township, not the Town: 13 percent of Montague's 2001 population lived in collective dwellings, against 2.5 percent in Smiths Falls. The staff were the Town's footprint: health and social assistance was 16 percent of its workforce in 2001.

helpIt cannot yet say

  • circleThe list of the 42, as a list. Statistics Canada never printed it. The 1976 directory names 42 facilities of the type, Rideau among them, and the 1977 directory names none, so the set is reconstructible, not published.
  • circleWhere the people went. The province reports about 1,000 moved out of the three facilities, 2004 to 2009, "close to their families and friends wherever possible", through about 80 agencies. No placement count by municipality is published; the ministry's own evaluation reports hold location data and are not public.
  • circleWhether the Centre's wind-down shaped today's population or housing. No study has tested that for any Canadian town of this size. The nearest case, Tranquille in Kamloops, is a 1983 plan to place about 80 of some 300 residents locally, about one in four; no count after closure exists.
  • circleA count that separates where people became homeless from where the programs are. The County's dashboard gives the Town 65 of 132 households on the by-name list (August 2026), with the Director's caveat; no published count separates the two.
Plain reading. The Committee's account of a town that carried more than its share holds, and held before 2009. What the record does not support is that the closure made it worse. First test, 20 October 2026; a sealed review follows with the written brief.
